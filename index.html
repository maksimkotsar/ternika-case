Challenge

Sell a community that doesn't exist yet

Design a website that sells the community instead of just showing renders, and sends the sales team warm leads with a clear request.

The audience is families aged 28–45 buying their first or a bigger home and comparing several developments at once. Off-plan buyers pay for something that hasn't been built, so the site's real job is trust — and answering four objections before the first call with sales:

“It's too far”

The community is outside the city center. Buyers don't know what's nearby.

“That unit isn't available”

Floor plans on developer websites rarely match what's actually for sale.

“Can I afford it?”

Prices and payment options are unclear.

“Will they finish it?”

The biggest fear in buying off-plan is trusting the developer.

Process

Logic first, visuals second

I built the page around the buyer's journey. Each section had to answer one specific buyer question before it got a design.

Emotion→Facts→Where is it→Which unit→How to pay→Who builds it→Request

A wireframe without color or images shows what matters: the order in which buyers get answers. The map and the catalog got the most space because they handle the “too far” and “not available” objections.

Key decisions

What the research led to

Developer websites all make the same promises. Here every promise is replaced with something the buyer can check for themselves.

01

A neighborhood map instead of “great amenities nearby”

Nobody believes “schools and shops nearby”. The map shows 300+ real places: schools, daycare, clinics, sports, cafés, malls. A “15 minutes on foot / by car” filter answers the “too far” objection on its own.

02

A catalog synced with the developer's CRM

Floor plans come from the sales team's inventory system and refresh every 15 minutes. Only available units are shown. A request from a unit card reaches the manager with the unit number, size and floor.

03

Different requests for different intents

Instead of one “Contact us” button: “Get floor plans”, “Get a payment quote”, “Get the brochure”, “Check the price”. The sales team instantly knows where each buyer is in the decision.

04

Money explained before the call

Full payment, 0% installments until completion, monthly plans from RUB 50,000, subsidized family mortgage from 6%, and a 2-month price lock. Buyers find their option and call asking “how do I sign up?”

05

Mobile first

71% of traffic is mobile. Callouts on the architecture renders never overlap even at 320 px, unit cards scroll one at a time, and the catalog uses lightweight plan previews.

06

Built on trust

Completion dates on the first screen. The finish standard as a clear list. The legal entity, tax ID and official project declaration in the open. Whatever buyers want to verify, they find on their own.

07

Engaging motion that holds attention

A development website is long, and the biggest risk is that people leave halfway. So the page tells the community's story through scroll: a star from the brand pattern (a nod to the blackthorn the community is named after) opens up to reveal the next scene, key numbers appear one by one, courtyard cards slide over the renders. The motion is scroll-driven, not time-based, so visitors set their own pace and never wait.

Design

How every section leads to a sale

Under each screen: why the section is there. The tags show what it works on:

Trust removes the fear of “will they finish it”
Desire makes people want to live here
Action removes the last barrier to a request

The motion isn't decoration

It's scroll-driven, tells the community's story step by step, and holds attention on a long page so visitors reach the floor plans and payment options.

Result

85 leads in the first month

The site handles the “too far”, “what's actually available”, “can I afford it” and “will they finish it” objections on its own — so the developer can run ads to cold audiences.
